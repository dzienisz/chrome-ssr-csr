Harbor lighthouse keepers kept careful weather journals

For nearly two centuries the keepers of the northern harbor lighthouse wrote down the wind, the swell and the colour of the sky every four hours.

Their journals survived floods, fires and two wars because each new keeper carried the stack of notebooks up the spiral stairs to the lamp room.

Historians now compare those pencil notes with modern satellite records and find that the old observations were surprisingly accurate about storm timing.

One keeper described a green flash at sunset so often that visiting sailors began to schedule their evening walks around his predictions.

The final entry, written the night before the light was automated, simply lists the barometer reading and thanks the gulls for their company.

  • Margaret wrote that her grandfather delivered paraffin to the lighthouse by rowing boat every second Tuesday.
  • Tomasz asked whether the journals had been digitised, because his students want to plot the barometer readings themselves.
  • A retired pilot remembered seeing the beam from forty miles out on clear winter nights above the frozen bay.
